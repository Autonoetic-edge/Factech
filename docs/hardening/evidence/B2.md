## 2. What the new policy version is

Name: **`pad-sequence-v3-guided`**. Selected only by
`HEAD_GATE_POLICY=pad-sequence-v3-guided`. Any other value, including empty,
`v3`, `1` and `true`, selects the frozen policy — tested.

Unchanged and copied verbatim: 12 frames at 1400 ms, the 15–20 s span, the
1100–1900 ms gaps, the 1600 ms reaction interval, the three-phase split, the
0.25 frontality limit, the 0.15 stillness limit, the 0.20 target, the
two-consecutive-frame hold, and the both-ratios-must-agree rule. No model, no
match threshold, no PAD threshold, no PAD decision rule, no capture timing and
no wire format was touched.

Exactly two decision rules differ.

**Rule 1 — stillness is judged per frame against the median, not as a span.**
v2-slow vetoes the scan if the peak-to-peak spread of the three settle frames
exceeds 0.15 on either ratio. v3 takes the same median baseline, keeps the same
`|baseline| <= 0.25` frontality check, and requires **at least two** settle
frames within **0.15** of that baseline on both ratios. One landmark outlier in
three samples no longer vetoes a head that is otherwise still. A head that never
settles still has fewer than two agreeing frames and still fails.

**Rule 2 — a late switch is tolerated only as a leading run.** Frames showing
the previous turn are ignored inside a window only if they come **before that
window's first valid frame**. An opposite-direction frame at or after the first
valid frame still fails `opposite_action_in_window`, exactly as before. This
tolerates a slow turner; it does not tolerate a wrong turn.

The duplication between `validate()` and `validate_guided()` is deliberate.
Sharing the phase-split code would have meant editing the frozen gate.

**Coarse retry hint.** The v3 result carries `hint`, one of `hold_still`,
`turn_sooner`, `turn_less_or_more`, `try_again`, or `None` on success. It carries
no score, no threshold, no yaw value, no PAD detail and no digit — asserted by
test. It reaches nothing outside the engine's own decision dict: `main.py` is not
approved and was not touched. See §6.

The v3 result also carries `policy` and `settle_inliers`. The frozen result
carries neither, so an unlabelled `head_sequence` block in a decision trace means
v2-slow ran — also asserted by test.

**Old 4 of 9. New 8 of 9.** 16, 18, 19 and 20 newly pass; 14 still fails; no

This matches the reviewer's independent hand check exactly. Nothing was tuned to
reach it: both rules were written from the B1 diagnosis and the table was
produced by the first run.

Why each one moved:

- **16, 19** — one settle frame disagreed on the mouth ratio by 0.1649 and
  0.1810; the other two agreed to within 0.05. Rule 1.
- **18** — one settle frame disagreed on the eye ratio by 0.1584 after two
  agreeing frames. Rule 1.
- **20** — the second window opened while the participant was still turned the
  first way; they then turned and held for the last two frames. The opposite
  frames all precede the first valid frame. Rule 2.
- **14 still fails, correctly.** The participant held the first direction
  through three of the four second-window frames and only turned back in the
  final frame. One valid frame is not a held turn under either policy. The
  failure moves from `not_frontal_and_still` to `action_order_or_rotation`, which
  is the honest reason, and the hint is `turn_sooner`.

### I cannot reach 90% on this set without loosening something

B2's development exit target is at least 90% first-attempt completion. **8 of 9
is 88.9%.** It is below the target and I am not going to make it pass.

The only remaining failure is scan 14, and it is a real one: that participant did
not complete the second turn inside the window. Reaching 9 of 9 would need the
hold shortened to a single frame, or the window widened past the capture, and
both weaken the gate against exactly the single-frame spikes and re-timed
captures in §5. The right answer for scan 14 is Phase 2's guidance work — a
countdown and a clear turn cue so the participant turns sooner — not a looser
rule.

Two further cautions on the 88.9%:

- Nine traces have a resolution of about 11 points. 8/9 and 9/9 are not
  meaningfully different numbers on this sample, and neither is a completion
  rate.
- **These same nine traces motivated both rules.** This is the tuning set. A
  development number measured on the set it was tuned on is a sanity check, not
  a result. The 90% gate should be judged on B4's held-out participants, per
  BIOMETRIC_TRACK.md.

Synthetic numbers replayed through `validate_guided()`. Each must fail under the

| trace | what it stands for | v3 result |

**What this does not show.** These are numbers. They are not a print, a screen, a
mask or an injected stream. Nothing here is evidence of spoof resistance; the
head gate is one bit of ordering evidence and the mandatory learned PAD is
separate and unchanged. Physical attack evidence is B5's job and has not been
collected.

## 10. Open questions

1. **Policy naming collision.** `anti_spoof.POLICY_VERSION` is the string
   `"pad-sequence-v2-slow"` and is what the decision's `pad.policy` field
   reports. If `HEAD_GATE_POLICY` selects v3, the PAD block would still stamp
   v2-slow while the head gate reports v3. Today that is only confusing;
   before any evaluation run it is a data-integrity problem, because B4/B5
   results are grouped by that string. `anti_spoof.py` has no row. Proposing one
   is the next governance step — should the stamp become a compound
   `pad=…/head=…`, or should v3 get a distinct top-level policy name?
2. **Is 88.9% acceptable as B2's development exit, given §3?** My reading is
   that the gate should be judged after Phase 2's guidance work and on B4's
   held-out set, not on nine tuning traces. That is a call for the reviewer.
3. `main.py` retry hint — blocked on the gateway team's answer in §6.
4. `apps/tests/face-guide.test.js` — row proposed in §7, not applied.
5. Phase 5's frames remain closed and unrequested.
