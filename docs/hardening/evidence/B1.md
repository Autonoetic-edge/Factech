Per trace: `id`, `subject` (S1–S5), `label`, `endpoint`, `policy`, `build_id`,

identifying, and it is what keeps the three incompatible engine generations from
being pooled — see section 4.

Five real subjects map to S1–S5 in first-seen order. The mapping is not recorded
anywhere in the workspace.

## 3. The replay, and why it proves faithfulness

The fixture stores the recorded yaw ratio pairs, not landmarks. The test rebuilds
a synthetic five-point layout whose real `app.head_sequence.yaw()` returns that
same pair (closed-form inversion; eyes on the x axis), so the replay drives the
**shipped `validate()` and the shipped `yaw()`**, not a stand-in. The round trip
is asserted to 1e-9 on three sample pairs.

All nine `pad-sequence-v2-slow` traces reproduce their recorded verdict exactly —
`ok`, `reason`, `phases` and `span_ms`:

|---|---|---|---|

| 14 | verify | `not_frontal_and_still` | yes |
| 15 | verify | (pass) | yes |
| 16 | enroll | `not_frontal_and_still` | yes |
| 17 | enroll | (pass) | yes |

### Rounding cannot have flipped a verdict

The recorded yaw pairs are rounded to four decimals, so the replay carries at
most about 1e-4 of reconstruction error. Measured distance from each trace to
the nearest decision boundary it is judged against:

| id | max abs baseline (limit 0.25) | settle peak-to-peak (limit 0.15) | window 1 best min-delta | window 2 best min-delta |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 13 | 0.2452 | 0.0657 | 1.3205 | 1.5788 |
| 14 | 0.2156 | **0.1909** | 1.1407 | 1.3923 |
| 15 | 0.1879 | 0.1292 | 1.4430 | 1.7221 |
| 16 | 0.1534 | **0.1649** | 1.1749 | 1.0482 |
| 17 | 0.1392 | 0.0881 | 1.5597 | 2.0851 |
| 18 | 0.1640 | **0.1760** | 2.1414 | 1.5192 |
| 19 | 0.1389 | **0.1810** | 1.2335 | 1.8012 |
| 20 | 0.1687 | 0.1010 | 0.7505 | 1.3542 |
| 21 | 0.0151 | 0.0837 | 0.7134 | 0.5701 |

The tightest margin anywhere is id 13's baseline at 0.2452 against the 0.25
limit — **0.0048, about 48x the worst-case rounding error**. No verdict sits
near enough to a boundary for the rounding to matter.

Two measured observations, both consistent with the briefing and neither acted
on in this phase:

- The four `not_frontal_and_still` rejections are peak-to-peak 0.1649–0.1909
  against a 0.15 limit — each is a near miss driven by one settle frame. The
  passing trace 15 is at 0.1292, so pass and fail are not well separated by this
  rule.
- Turn magnitudes are 0.57–2.09 against a target of 0.20, i.e. people turn
  roughly 3–10x further than the gate asks for.

## 6. Unverified

- **The 100 skipped tests are unverified in this run.** Not run, not claimed.
- **The fixture's faithfulness rests on the engine's own recorded trace.** The
  replay proves the current `validate()` agrees with what the engine recorded at
  capture time, given the recorded yaw. It does **not** re-derive yaw from the
  JPEG frames, so it cannot detect an error in detection or landmarking upstream
  of `yaw()`. Re-deriving would require opening the frames, which no phase has
  approved. Unverified.
- Exactly how the gateway's `frame_meta` timestamps relate to the timestamps
  inside `scan.msgpack` was checked only by confirming `span_ms` matches the
  recorded value on every replayed trace. Beyond that, unverified.
- **These nine traces are development data from four people on one device in
  daylight.** They establish nothing about recognition accuracy, false match
  rates or spoof resistance, and they are not a validation set. The head
  challenge remains worth roughly one bit; the learned PAD is the real defence,
  and it is a candidate, not validated protection.
